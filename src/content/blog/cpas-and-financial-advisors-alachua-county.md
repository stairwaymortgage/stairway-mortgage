## The Income Documentation Challenge Is Real for Your Profession

Most mortgage processes are designed around W-2 employees with predictable, salaried pay. You may not fit that mold.

If you operate your own CPA firm or independent advisory practice, your income likely flows through an S-corp, partnership, or sole proprietorship. You may take a modest salary and draw the rest as distributions. You may write off a significant portion of your gross revenue as legitimate business expenses — which is smart tax planning, but it reduces the net income a lender sees on your returns.

For financial advisors whose earnings include a mix of retainer fees, performance bonuses, or trailing commissions, the picture grows more complex. Lenders often want to see a two-year average of that variable income, and they may treat declining income differently than income that is flat or trending upward.

None of this means homeownership or a strategic property purchase is out of reach. It means the preparation stage matters more for you than it does for a salaried employee.

## What AI and Shifting Compensation Trends Mean for Your File

There is a real conversation happening in financial services right now about how compensation is evolving. Firms are increasingly rewarding specialized skills, including technology fluency, with higher pay — but that pay often takes the form of bonuses, equity, or variable incentives rather than base salary increases. If your own compensation or that of an associate you are advising has shifted in this direction recently, it is worth knowing how lenders will interpret that history.

A bonus received once may be treated as non-recurring. A pattern of bonuses over two years is often treated as part of your qualifying income. The timing of when you apply, relative to when your most recent tax return was filed, can work for or against you depending on how your income looks year over year.

## Strategies Worth Discussing Before You Apply

Because you understand financial planning, you will appreciate that mortgage strategy is most effective when it starts well before the application.

**Timing around your tax returns.** If you are between filing years and your most recent return shows lower income due to heavy deductions, it may be worth exploring whether a bank statement program or a CPA-letter approach could supplement your file. These are not universal solutions, but they exist for exactly your situation.

**Entity structure and how lenders read it.** A lender reviewing an S-corp return will often add back certain deductions and look at your ownership percentage to determine your share of business income. If you have recently restructured your entity, that transition period may require additional explanation.

**Down payment sourcing.** If your liquidity is invested — in client accounts you manage, in real estate you already own, or in retirement vehicles — documenting that it is accessible without triggering unnecessary tax consequences is a conversation worth having before you go under contract on a property.
