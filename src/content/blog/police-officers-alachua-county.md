## Why Law Enforcement Income Can Complicate Things

Most mortgage lenders want to see stable, predictable income. The challenge for many police officers is that a meaningful portion of their take-home pay may come from overtime shifts, court appearance pay, or off-duty details. That income is real and consistent, but it may not be treated the same way base salary is.

In most cases, a lender will average your overtime over a 24-month period using pay stubs and W-2 forms. If you have worked steady overtime at the Alachua County Sheriff's Office or within the Gainesville Police Department for two years or more, that history can often be included in your qualifying income.

This matters because your qualifying income directly affects how much you may be able to borrow. Getting clarity on this number early helps you shop in a realistic range and avoids surprises later in the process.

## Loan Programs That May Work for Police Officers

There is no single loan program designed exclusively for police officers at the federal level, but several programs are often a strong fit for the profile that law enforcement borrowers bring.

**FHA Loans** — These are government-backed loans that allow down payments as low as 3.5% for eligible buyers. Credit score requirements are generally more flexible than conventional loans, which can help officers who are earlier in their financial picture.

**Conventional Loans** — If your credit profile is solid and you have been building savings, a conventional loan may allow you to avoid mortgage insurance with a sufficient down payment. These loans often suit officers who are further along in their careers.

**USDA Rural Development Loans** — This is worth a closer look if you are drawn to properties outside Gainesville's developed core. Parts of Alachua County fall within USDA-eligible zones, which could mean no down payment required for qualified borrowers. Eligibility depends on both the property location and your household income.

**Florida Bond Programs** — Florida Housing Finance Corporation offers first-time homebuyer programs that include down payment and closing cost assistance. Officers purchasing their first home in Alachua County may find these worth exploring. Availability and terms change, so current information matters.

## Getting Your Documentation Ready

One area where officers often feel unprepared is documentation. The nature of law enforcement pay — with multiple income sources, union dues, and sometimes multiple employers for off-duty work — can make a file look complicated at first glance.

Having the following ready early often smooths the process considerably:

- Two years of W-2s or tax returns
- Recent pay stubs covering at least 30 days
- Documentation of any off-duty employment income
- Bank statements for the past two to three months
- A current record of any outstanding debts

If you have questions about what your specific situation will look like to a lender, talking through it before you apply is often the most useful thing you can do. It gives you a realistic picture without any commitment.
