## When Orders Arrive Before You're Ready

You open the envelope, or more likely the email, and the orders are official. You have a reporting date, a new duty station, and a house full of decisions to make. If you own your current home, you are thinking about whether to sell or rent it out. If you have been renting, you are wondering whether this move is finally the right time to buy. Either way, the clock is already running.

PCS moves are not like civilian relocations. You do not get to time the housing market. You do not get to wait for the ideal moment. You go when the military says go, and your mortgage strategy has to be built around that reality.

This is where the VA home loan benefit, if you have earned it through service, may be one of the most useful tools available to you. Understanding how it works before the orders come, rather than after, puts you in a much stronger position to act quickly and with confidence.

## What the VA Loan Benefit Actually Offers Military Buyers

The VA loan program was designed with service members in mind, and several of its features align directly with the challenges of a PCS move.

**No down payment required in most cases.** When you are relocating every two to three years, accumulating a large down payment is genuinely difficult. The VA loan program removes that obstacle for eligible borrowers, meaning you may be able to purchase a home without depleting your savings.

**No private mortgage insurance.** Conventional loans typically require PMI when a borrower puts down less than 20%. VA loans do not carry this requirement, which may reduce your monthly housing cost meaningfully.

**Flexible qualification standards.** VA lenders, including our team at Stairway Mortgage, are experienced with the documentation that comes with military income, including base pay, BAH, BAS, and special pays. These income sources are well understood within the VA lending process.

**Assumability.** A VA loan can often be assumed by a future buyer, which may become relevant when you receive your next set of orders and need to sell quickly.

## BAH, Pay Changes, and Planning Around Military Income

One reality that many military families are navigating right now is the uncertainty around housing allowances and pay. Discussions at the federal level around BAH accuracy and military compensation underscore something service members already know: your income picture can shift with each set of orders.

BAH is tied to your duty station and dependent status, which means a PCS move often changes the number you are working with. That matters when a lender is calculating what you may qualify for.

## The Timeline Problem: Moving Fast Without Moving Blind

PCS moves often come with short timelines. You may have 30 to 60 days from orders to reporting date, and during that window you need to find housing, arrange the move, and if buying, navigate the entire mortgage process.

Here is how military families tend to get through it successfully.

**Get pre-approved before you travel to the new duty station.** Having a pre-approval letter in hand when you are touring homes, whether in person or virtually, allows you to move quickly when you find the right property. Sellers take pre-approved buyers seriously.

**Work with a lender who understands military timelines.** Not every lender is equipped to move at the pace a PCS requires. At Stairway Mortgage, our team is familiar with the urgency involved and works to keep the process moving without cutting corners.

**Know your entitlement status before you shop.** If you have used a VA loan before, your entitlement may be fully restored, partially available, or tied up in a current property. Understanding this before you start shopping prevents surprises.

**Consider the full cost of ownership in the new location.** Property taxes, homeowners insurance, and HOA fees vary significantly by region. Our [mortgage calculators](/calculators/) can help you factor in these costs alongside your loan payment.

## If You Are Buying and Selling at the Same Time

This is one of the most common and most stressful situations military homeowners face. You receive orders, you own a home at your current station, and you need to figure out what to do with it while also securing housing at the new one.

You have a few paths.

**Sell before you leave.** If the timeline allows and the market cooperates, selling your current home before reporting gives you the clearest financial picture going into the new purchase.

**Rent it out.** Some military families choose to convert their current home into a rental, particularly if they are underwater on the loan or the rental market in that area is strong. This adds landlord responsibilities, but it can preserve equity over time.

**Use remaining VA entitlement for a second simultaneous loan.** Depending on your loan balances and county loan limits, you may have enough entitlement to carry two VA loans at the same time. This is worth discussing in detail with a loan officer who understands VA entitlement calculations.

Every situation is different, and there is no single path that works for every family. What matters is reviewing your specific numbers with someone who knows the program well.
