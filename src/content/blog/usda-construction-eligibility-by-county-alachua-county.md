## What Is a USDA Construction-to-Permanent Loan?

A USDA construction-to-permanent loan combines two things that are usually handled separately: the short-term financing used to fund building costs and the long-term mortgage that replaces it once construction is complete.

Instead of closing twice and paying two sets of costs, you close once. The loan covers construction draws as your builder hits agreed-upon milestones, and then it converts automatically to a standard mortgage when your certificate of occupancy is issued.

For eligible borrowers, this structure could reduce the upfront financial burden significantly. You may also be able to roll in certain closing costs, depending on the appraised value. ## Who May Qualify for This Program?

USDA construction loans are designed for low-to-moderate income households, and there are several factors that go into eligibility.

**Property requirements:**
- Must be located in a USDA-eligible rural area
- Must be intended as your primary residence
- Must meet USDA property condition and construction standards
- The builder must be approved and licensed

**Borrower requirements:**
- Income must fall at or below the USDA's county-level household income limits for Alachua County
- Must have a qualifying credit profile (no specific minimum is universal, but lenders typically look at your full credit picture)
- Must demonstrate repayment ability through documented income
- Must be a U.S. citizen, qualified alien, or non-citizen national

The income limits for Alachua County are set by the USDA and take household size into account. A single-person household has a different ceiling than a family of five. This is worth checking early, especially if multiple earners in your household contribute income.

## The Construction Process: What to Expect

Building a home is not like buying one, and the financing reflects that. Here is a general sequence for how a USDA construction-to-permanent loan works once you are approved.

**Step 1: Lot and builder selection.** You choose your land and a builder who meets USDA's requirements. The builder must be licensed, insured, and willing to work within the program's draw structure.

**Step 2: Plans and appraisal.** Your construction plans are submitted, and an appraiser provides an estimate of the home's value based on those plans. This determines how much you may be able to borrow.

**Step 3: Loan closing.** You close on the loan before any construction begins. From this point, the lender manages draws — releasing funds to your builder as each phase of construction is completed and inspected.

**Step 4: Conversion.** Once construction is complete and you receive your certificate of occupancy, the loan converts to a permanent mortgage. You begin making standard mortgage payments at that point.

## Why Tariff Pressures Make Loan Structure More Important Now

Material costs for new construction have been fluctuating, and there is ongoing industry discussion about how tariffs on imported building materials could push project costs higher. This makes the structure of your financing more important than ever.

With a construction-to-permanent loan, your loan terms are established at closing — before construction begins. That means you are not scrambling for new financing mid-project if circumstances change. For buyers in eligible parts of Alachua County who are serious about building, locking in a clear, single-close structure could offer a degree of predictability in an otherwise uncertain cost environment.

This is one reason why programs like USDA construction financing have drawn renewed attention among first-time buyers who want to build in rural and semi-rural areas without high upfront costs.
