## What a Renovation Loan Actually Is

A renovation loan is a mortgage product that lets you finance home improvements as part of a single loan. Instead of taking out a separate personal loan or drawing on a line of credit, you wrap the cost of the work into your home financing.

There are a few common structures:

- **FHA 203(k)** — This government-backed option may be used to purchase and renovate a home at the same time, or to refinance an existing mortgage and roll in renovation costs. There are two versions: a Streamlined option for smaller projects and a Standard option for larger structural work.
- **Fannie Mae HomeStyle** — A conventional renovation loan that may allow a broader range of project types, including luxury upgrades, as long as they become a permanent part of the property.
- **USDA Renovation Loans** — For properties in eligible rural zones, certain USDA programs may include renovation components. Parts of Alachua County qualify as USDA-eligible areas, which could make this relevant for homeowners outside the Gainesville urban core.

Each program has its own eligibility criteria, loan limits, and contractor requirements. Exploring [our renovation loan program overview](/loan-programs/renovation/) is a good way to understand which structure fits your situation.

## What the Process Looks Like

Renovation loans involve a few more moving parts than a standard purchase or refinance. Here is a general overview of how they tend to work:

1. **You identify your project scope.** The more specific you can be, the smoother the process. Get contractor estimates before you apply if possible.
2. **You apply for the loan.** The loan amount is based on the projected value of your home after the improvements, not just its current value.
3. **The lender reviews the plans and appraises the future value.** An appraiser will look at what your home is likely to be worth once the work is done.
4. **Funds are held in escrow.** As the work progresses, funds are released in draws tied to completed milestones.
5. **You make one monthly payment.** The mortgage and renovation costs are combined into a single payment structure.

## Common Questions Alachua County Homeowners Ask

**Can I choose my own contractor?**
In most cases, yes — but the contractor typically needs to be licensed and approved by the lender. For FHA 203(k) loans especially, the contractor must meet specific requirements. Starting that search early saves time.

**What if my home has structural issues?**
The FHA 203(k) Standard program may be structured to handle structural repairs, foundation work, and larger rehabilitation projects. It is more involved than the Streamlined version, but it may be the right fit for homes needing significant attention.

**What if I am still paying off my current mortgage?**
Renovation refinancing is designed for exactly this scenario. You refinance your existing loan and roll in the cost of improvements. The result is a single loan that addresses both your current balance and your planned project.
