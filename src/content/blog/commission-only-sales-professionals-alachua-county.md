## When Your Paycheck Looks Different Every Month

You close a strong quarter and your income reflects it. Then the next month brings pipeline delays, a deal that falls through, and a much quieter deposit hitting your account. That variability is the reality of commission-only sales work — and it is also the exact thing that makes mortgage lenders pause.

If you work in sales in Alachua County, whether you are based in Gainesville or in one of the smaller communities surrounding it, you have likely heard that getting a mortgage on variable income is complicated. It does not have to be impossible. What it does require is understanding how lenders read your financial picture and preparing accordingly.

This guide is written specifically for commission-only professionals navigating that process in Alachua County.

## Why Standard Mortgage Guidelines Create Friction for Sales Earners

Most mortgage underwriting is built around predictable, salaried income. When a W-2 employee applies, a lender can look at a pay stub and project annual earnings with confidence. Commission-only earners do not fit that model.

Instead, lenders look at your last two years of tax returns, calculate a monthly average, and use that figure to determine what you may qualify to borrow. That process creates two common friction points for sales professionals:

**Tax deductions that reduce qualifying income.** Many sales professionals write off business expenses — mileage, client meals, home office costs — which lowers taxable income. That is smart tax strategy, but it can also reduce the income number a lender is allowed to count.

**Income gaps or downward trends.** If year two earnings were lower than year one, underwriters may use the lower figure or raise questions about stability. Even if this year is your strongest ever, what is documented in your returns carries more weight than what you project.

Understanding these dynamics early gives you time to plan around them.

---

## What Documentation Commission-Only Borrowers Typically Need

Lenders will generally want to see a consistent package of income documentation. While every situation differs, commission-only borrowers in Alachua County should typically be prepared to provide:

- Two years of personal federal tax returns, all schedules included
- Two years of W-2s or 1099s depending on your employment structure
- Year-to-date profit-and-loss statement if you are also self-employed
- Verification of employment from your current employer
- Recent bank statements showing deposit history

If your income has grown over the two-year period, that trend can work in your favor. Underwriters often view increasing commission earnings as a sign of a stable and growing sales career.

## Loan Programs That May Work for Commission-Only Earners in Alachua County

Alachua County has a diverse housing landscape. Gainesville, as the county seat, offers urban and suburban properties ranging from historic neighborhoods to newer developments near the University of Florida. Outside Gainesville, the county includes more rural areas — some of which fall within USDA Rural Development eligibility zones.

That geographic range matters because it expands the loan programs that may apply to you:

**Conventional loans** remain a common path for commission earners with strong credit profiles and documented two-year income histories. Down payments can vary depending on loan size and lender requirements.

**FHA loans** may offer a workable path for buyers earlier in their savings journey, with down payments typically starting at 3.5% of the purchase price for qualifying borrowers.

**USDA Rural Development loans** could be relevant if you are purchasing in a qualifying area outside Gainesville. These loans are geography-dependent, so verifying eligibility for a specific address is an important step.

## How Recent Changes to Real Estate Commissions Affect Your Transaction

The way real estate commissions are negotiated and disclosed has shifted in recent years. For sales professionals who are also purchasing a home, this shift is worth paying attention to from both sides of the transaction.

Buyer's agent compensation is increasingly negotiated directly between buyers and their agents rather than flowing automatically through the seller. For you as a buyer, this means understanding those arrangements upfront so there are no surprises at the closing table.

For your mortgage, commission paid to a buyer's agent is generally not part of your loan amount — it is a separate transaction cost you may need to factor into your overall budget. Planning for this alongside your down payment and closing costs is part of a thorough purchase strategy.

## Building Your Qualifying Profile Before You Apply

The most effective thing a commission-only sales professional can do is prepare well in advance of a purchase — ideally six to twelve months ahead. Here is what that preparation often looks like:

**Review your last two tax returns.** If your deductions have significantly reduced your qualifying income, you may want to work with a tax professional on whether future returns should be structured differently. There is always a balance between tax efficiency and documented income for mortgage purposes.

**Track your year-to-date earnings carefully.** A strong current-year income trajectory can be part of the story you present to an underwriter, even if it cannot be fully counted until it appears on a return.

**Stabilize credit and reduce revolving debt.** Your debt-to-income ratio is calculated using documented income. Lowering monthly obligations gives you more room within that ratio.

**Maintain clean bank statements.** Large, unexplained deposits can trigger underwriting questions. Keeping records of where every significant deposit originated saves time during the review process.
