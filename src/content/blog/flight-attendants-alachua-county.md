You board flights before most people wake up, cross time zones in a single shift, and come home to a schedule that changes every month. When you decide you want to buy a home in Alachua County, Florida, that lifestyle creates a very specific challenge: your income does not look like a standard nine-to-five salary, and many lenders do not know what to do with it.

Per-diem allowances, trip-based pay, reserve versus line-holder status, and seasonal fluctuations all show up in your financial profile in ways that can confuse an underwriter unfamiliar with the aviation industry. That is why working with someone who understands your world matters.

## Why Income Documentation Is the Central Challenge

Most mortgage programs ask for two years of income history. For flight attendants, that history might include multiple airlines, a furlough period, a move from part-time to full-time status, or a shift from regional to mainline flying. Each of those transitions requires clear documentation and a lender who knows how to read it.

Recent hiring waves across major carriers have brought a new wave of flight attendants into their first or second full year of employment. If you are newer to the role, your income documentation window may still be building. That does not necessarily close the door — it just shapes which loan programs make the most sense for your situation right now.

Key documents you will likely need include:

- Two years of W-2s or 1099s (or a combination)
- Recent pay stubs covering at least 30 days
- A verification of employment from your airline's HR department
- Bank statements showing consistent deposits
- Documentation of any per-diem amounts if you want them considered

Per-diem pay is generally non-taxable, so it does not appear on your W-2. Some loan programs allow it to be counted separately with proper employer documentation. ## Loan Programs Flight Attendants Often Use

There is no single loan that works for every flight attendant, but several programs tend to be a strong fit depending on your situation:

**Conventional loans** are often used by flight attendants with two or more years at one airline and solid W-2 documentation. Down payment requirements can start at 3% for eligible first-time buyers.

**FHA loans** offer a more flexible approach to credit history and may suit flight attendants who are earlier in their careers or managing some financial complexity from time away from flying.

**USDA loans** could be an option if the home you are buying falls in an eligible rural or semi-rural area of Alachua County. This program is worth exploring if you want to minimize upfront costs.

**Bank statement loans** exist for flight attendants whose income is harder to document through traditional means — particularly those with significant per-diem income or self-employed secondary income. These are specialty programs, but they are real options.

## How to Prepare Before You Start

The single most useful thing you can do before reaching out to a lender is to get your documents in order. Pull your last two years of tax returns. Download your most recent pay stubs. Check your credit report for any errors that should be corrected now rather than mid-process.

If your credit history has gaps or your score is lower than you would like, there are often steps you can take over several months to strengthen your profile before applying. A short conversation with our team can help you understand where you stand and what — if anything — to work on first.
