## Why Variable Income Complicates the Standard Process

Most mortgage underwriting systems were designed with a W-2 employee in mind. A salary that stays the same each pay period is easy to verify and project forward. Commission income, bonuses, and override structures are all legitimate and can be substantial — but they require more documentation to establish reliability.

Lenders typically look for a two-year history of variable income to establish a baseline. They will average those two years to arrive at a monthly qualifying figure. If your income went up significantly in year two, that trend is noted, but the average is usually what drives the calculation.

For account executives in Alachua County, this means your tax returns carry real weight. If you take aggressive deductions, your adjusted gross income on paper may look lower than what you actually deposited into your bank account. That gap is worth understanding before you start shopping for a home.

## Documents That Tell Your Income Story

Preparing your documentation thoughtfully can smooth the underwriting process considerably. Here is what you will typically need:

**Two years of personal tax returns (all pages).** Underwriters want the full picture, not just the summary page.

**W-2s and 1099s from the past two years.** If you work as a W-2 employee who earns commission, you need both. If you receive 1099 income, lenders treat that more like self-employment.

**Recent pay stubs showing year-to-date earnings.** These help confirm that income is ongoing and active.

**A written verification of employment.** Your employer will usually complete a form confirming your position, commission structure, and likelihood of continued employment.

**Bank statements (often two to three months).** These can help show actual cash flow when the tax return picture is complicated by deductions.

If your commission is genuinely your primary income source, taking the time to [explore loan programs](/loan-programs/) designed with non-traditional income in mind is worth doing early in the process.

## USDA, Conventional, and Other Options in Alachua County

Alachua County has a geographic split that matters for mortgage programs. Gainesville itself is an urban hub with a large university population and established housing stock. But move outside the city limits and you will find areas that qualify as rural under USDA guidelines — which could open access to programs that do not require a down payment.

For account executives whose income documentation is solid but who want to preserve cash reserves, a USDA loan in an eligible part of Alachua County may be worth discussing. Income limits and property eligibility rules apply, so location matters.

Conventional loans remain a strong path for many commission-earning buyers, particularly those with a two-year earnings history and a down payment ready. FHA loans offer flexibility on credit and down payment — typically starting around 3.5% down — and can work well when income documentation is straightforward even if it is variable.
