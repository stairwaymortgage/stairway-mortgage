## What a Reverse Mortgage Actually Is

A reverse mortgage is a loan available to homeowners 62 and older that allows you to convert a portion of your home's equity into usable funds — without selling the home or taking on a monthly mortgage payment you have to make out of pocket.

Instead of you making payments to a lender, the loan balance grows over time. The loan is repaid when you sell the home, move out permanently, or pass away. At that point, any remaining equity after the loan is repaid belongs to you or your heirs.

The most common type is the Home Equity Conversion Mortgage, or HECM, which is backed by the Federal Housing Administration. This federal backing includes specific consumer protections that are worth understanding before you move forward.

## How the Funds Can Be Structured

One of the practical advantages of a HECM reverse mortgage is flexibility in how you receive the funds. Depending on your needs and your situation, you may be able to choose from:

- **A lump sum** — a single disbursement at closing
- **A line of credit** — available to draw from as needed
- **Monthly payments** — a set amount deposited regularly
- **A combination** of the above

## What You Need to Know Before Applying

A reverse mortgage is not a product you can apply for without some preparation, and that is by design. Here are the key requirements and steps:

**You must be 62 or older.** All borrowers on the title must meet the age requirement.

**The home must be your primary residence.** Vacation homes and investment properties are not eligible.

**A HUD-approved counseling session is required.** Before you can apply for a HECM, you must complete a session with an independent, HUD-approved housing counselor. This is not optional — it is a federal requirement, and it exists to make sure you fully understand the product before committing.

**You remain responsible for property taxes, insurance, and maintenance.** This is where some homeowners run into difficulty. The loan does not cover these ongoing costs, and failing to keep up with them could put the loan in jeopardy.

**Your heirs have options.** When the loan becomes due, heirs can repay the loan balance and keep the home, sell the home and use the proceeds to settle the debt, or walk away if the balance exceeds the home's value — because HECMs are non-recourse loans, meaning no one owes more than the home is worth at sale.

## Is a Reverse Mortgage Right for You?

A reverse mortgage is not a one-size-fits-all solution. It works well for some homeowners and is not the right fit for others. It tends to align well with situations like these:

- You plan to remain in your Alachua County home long-term
- Your home is paid off or nearly paid off
- You want to reduce financial strain without selling or downsizing
- You need a way to cover healthcare costs or home modifications
- You have considered your estate goals and discussed them with family

It may be less suitable if you plan to move in the near future, if leaving the home's full value to heirs is a top priority, or if ongoing costs like taxes and insurance are already difficult to manage.

The only way to know which category you fall into is to have a real conversation — one that looks at your specific property, your equity, your income, and your goals.
