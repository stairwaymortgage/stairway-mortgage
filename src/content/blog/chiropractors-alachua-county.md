## How Lenders Read Chiropractic Income

Most mortgage underwriters look at your adjusted gross income from Schedule C or your share of business income from an S-corp or partnership return. If your practice deducts equipment, malpractice insurance, staff salaries, and continuing education costs — all reasonable business expenses — your taxable income shrinks.

That shrinkage is fine for the IRS. It creates a challenge when a lender calculates your debt-to-income ratio, because they are working from that reduced number.

What this means practically:

- A chiropractor earning strong gross revenue may appear under-qualified on paper
- Two years of tax returns are typically required, and both years matter
- Significant income swings between years can trigger additional underwriting scrutiny

## Loan Programs That May Work for Chiropractors

There is no single loan product designed exclusively for chiropractors, but several programs may align well with how your income is structured.

**Conventional loans with self-employed documentation** remain a viable path for many practice owners who can demonstrate two years of stable or growing income, even after deductions.

**Bank statement loans** are a non-QM product that calculates qualifying income from deposit history rather than tax returns. If your deposits consistently reflect strong revenue, this could produce a higher qualifying income figure than your returns suggest.

**Professional mortgage programs** — sometimes called doctor loans — are offered by certain lenders to licensed healthcare providers. Chiropractors may be eligible depending on the lender's definition of qualifying professions. These programs sometimes allow smaller down payments and may view student loan debt differently.

**USDA Rural Development loans** may be available if you are looking at property in the less densely developed parts of Alachua County. While Gainesville itself is unlikely to qualify, surrounding communities and rural parcels sometimes fall within eligible zones. This program can allow for no-down-payment financing for eligible borrowers who meet income requirements.

## Preparing Your Application as a Practice Owner

The most effective thing you can do before applying is organize your financial documentation carefully. Lenders will typically want:

- Two years of personal federal tax returns (all schedules)
- Two years of business returns if you operate an LLC, S-corp, or partnership
- Year-to-date profit and loss statement prepared by a CPA
- Two to three months of business and personal bank statements
- Documentation of any business debt that does not appear on your personal credit report

If you pay yourself through distributions rather than a salary, be prepared to explain that structure. If your income grew significantly from year one to year two, that trend can actually work in your favor — lenders often average the two years, and an upward trajectory signals stability.

For chiropractors who are employed by a hospital, clinic, or group practice rather than operating their own business, the process is often more straightforward. W-2 income with consistent hours tends to document cleanly, and you may have access to a broader range of standard loan programs.
