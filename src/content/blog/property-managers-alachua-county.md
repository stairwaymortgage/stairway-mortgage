## The Financing Gap Most Property Managers Face

Here is the challenge many property managers run into: your income looks different on paper than a salaried employee's income. You may earn through a management company, hold an LLC, or collect income from multiple sources. Some of that income is predictable; some fluctuates by season.

Conventional lenders often struggle with these income profiles. But there are loan programs designed with investors and self-employed professionals in mind. DSCR loans, for example, evaluate a property based on its rental income relative to its debt obligations — rather than leaning heavily on your personal tax returns. Bank statement programs may allow lenders to look at your actual deposits rather than adjusted net income.

## Building a Portfolio Intentionally

Owning one rental property in Alachua County is a start. Building a portfolio that generates lasting passive income requires a more deliberate approach.

Some questions worth thinking through before your next acquisition:

**How much equity do you have in existing properties?** A cash-out refinance on a property you already own could provide capital for a down payment on the next one. This kind of equity recycling is a common strategy among experienced investors — and it is worth modeling before you assume you need outside capital.

**What is your reserve situation?** Most investment property loan programs require proof of liquid reserves — funds you could access in an emergency. The more properties you hold, the more lenders will typically want to see set aside.

**Are you thinking in terms of units or cash flow?** A four-unit property in Gainesville, for instance, is still eligible for certain residential loan programs rather than commercial financing. That distinction affects your rate structure, down payment requirements, and qualification process.

## What to Prepare Before You Apply

If you are thinking about purchasing an investment property in Alachua County within the next six to twelve months, a few steps now will put you in a much stronger position when the right property comes up.

- **Organize your income documentation.** Two years of tax returns, business bank statements, and any 1099s are typically the starting point. If your income is complex, a mortgage professional can tell you early which programs are most likely to work.

- **Pull your credit and review it.** Investment property loans often have stricter credit requirements than primary residence loans. Knowing where you stand gives you time to address anything that could affect your options.

- **Map out your down payment source.** Investment property purchases typically require a larger down payment than primary residences. Knowing whether that comes from savings, a cash-out on an existing property, or another source helps determine your timeline.

- **Think about your entity structure.** Some investors hold properties in LLCs. This can affect how you take title and which loan programs are available. Worth discussing early.
