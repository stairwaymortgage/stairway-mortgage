## Why Standard Mortgage Underwriting Often Falls Short for Surgeons

Conventional mortgage guidelines were built around a straightforward income profile: two years of W-2 employment, modest debt, a down payment saved over time. That model does not describe most surgeons entering the housing market.

Here is where the friction often appears:

**Student loan debt.** Standard underwriting typically counts your full student loan obligation against your debt-to-income ratio. For a surgeon carrying $300,000 or more in educational debt, that calculation alone can push you past the threshold many lenders use, even if your salary comfortably supports the home you want to buy.

**Short income history.** Lenders usually want to see two years of consistent earnings at your current income level. A surgeon who recently transitioned from a residency salary to an attending contract may have only months of documentation at the new pay scale.

**Limited cash reserves.** Years of training on a modest stipend do not leave much room to save a traditional down payment, especially while managing loan repayment.

Physician loan programs exist specifically to address these realities. They are not a workaround — they are a lending category built for high-earning professionals whose financial profile does not fit a conventional mold.

## What a Physician Loan Could Offer Surgeons

Physician loans are portfolio products offered by certain lenders and are underwritten with medical career trajectories in mind. While every program has its own guidelines, several features appear consistently across the options Jim Blackburn, NMLS #1072866, works with on behalf of surgeon clients.

**Flexible down payment options.** Many physician loan programs allow down payments significantly below the conventional standard, and some may offer options that require as little as 0% to 5% down depending on the loan amount and the lender's criteria.

**No private mortgage insurance.** Even with a smaller down payment, many physician programs waive the PMI requirement that would normally apply. That could keep your monthly payment more manageable during the years when you are paying down training debt and building savings simultaneously.

**Student loan treatment.** Some programs exclude deferred student loans from the debt-to-income calculation or use a reduced figure for loans on income-driven repayment plans. This single adjustment often changes the entire picture for surgeons.

**Employment contract acceptance.** If you have a signed contract for a new attending position, several physician loan programs will accept that document in place of pay stubs, allowing you to close on a home before your first paycheck arrives.
