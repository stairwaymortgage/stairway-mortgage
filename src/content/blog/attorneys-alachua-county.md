## Why Attorney Compensation Creates Underwriting Friction

The legal profession is in a period of real structural change. Private equity is reshaping how some large firms operate and how partners are compensated. Pay arrangements are being renegotiated at firms of all sizes. For attorneys on the move — whether lateraling, launching an independent practice, or transitioning between compensation models — this creates documentation challenges that a standard mortgage underwriter may not immediately understand.

Here is what tends to come up:

**Variable or contingency-based income.** If a meaningful portion of your earnings comes from case settlements or bonuses tied to performance, lenders may average that income over two years or discount it entirely if it appears irregular.

**Self-employment deductions.** A solo or small-firm attorney who properly deducts office space, staff, bar fees, and continuing education may show a net income on paper that does not reflect actual cash flow.

**Partnership draws.** If you receive a draw rather than a W-2 salary, underwriters will want to see firm financials in addition to your personal tax returns.

**Recent income changes.** A lateral move that increased your pay, or a shift from a salaried associate to an equity partner, may not yet be reflected in two years of returns — which is often what lenders require.

None of these situations makes homeownership out of reach. It does mean that how your file is structured and presented matters significantly.

## Preparing Your File: A Practical Checklist for Attorneys

Regardless of your compensation structure, there are steps you can take now to put your mortgage application in a strong position.

**Organize two years of tax returns.** Both personal and business returns will be required. If your income has increased significantly, be prepared to explain the trajectory.

**Request a year-to-date profit and loss statement.** If you are self-employed or a partner, this document helps underwriters understand income that has not yet appeared on a tax return.

**Review your student loan status.** Income-based repayment plans affect how your debt-to-income ratio is calculated. Know your current monthly payment figure before you apply.

**Avoid large undocumented deposits.** Lenders will scrutinize bank statements. Large case settlements or transfers should be documented clearly.

**Think about timing.** If you recently made partner or changed firms, a short waiting period — or a lender with flexible seasoning requirements — could work in your favor.
