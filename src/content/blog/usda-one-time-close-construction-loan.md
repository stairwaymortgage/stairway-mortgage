That situation is exactly what the USDA One-Time Close Construction Loan was designed for. If the property is in an eligible rural or suburban area — and more of Florida qualifies than most people realize — this program could let you build a new home with zero down. Understanding how it works is the first step toward making it real.

## What Makes This Loan Different

Most construction financing involves two separate transactions: a short-term construction loan to fund the build, and then a permanent mortgage once the home is finished. That means two applications, two rounds of underwriting, two sets of closing costs, and two chances for something to go sideways.

The USDA One-Time Close combines both into a single loan with a single closing. You lock in your permanent financing before the first nail is driven. When construction is complete, the loan simply converts to your regular monthly mortgage — no requalification, no second closing, no surprise.

## The Zero-Down Piece — and What It Actually Means

USDA loans are one of the few remaining programs that allow eligible borrowers to finance the full value of the home with zero down payment. In a construction context, that means the cost of building the home — labor, materials, contractor fees — can potentially be financed without a large cash contribution from you upfront.

Zero down does not mean zero costs. You will still encounter expenses like appraisal fees, title work, inspections, and in some cases prepaid items. Some of those costs may be rolled into the loan or covered by seller concessions, depending on how the transaction is structured. The zero-down feature is particularly meaningful right now, as affordability remains a real challenge for households trying to build wealth through homeownership. Programs like this one exist precisely to give working families a path forward that does not require years of saving before breaking ground.

## Who May Be Eligible

USDA eligibility has two main components: the property location and the borrower's household income.

**Property location:** The home must be built in a USDA-designated rural or eligible suburban area. Large portions of Florida outside of core metro zones qualify. If you are eyeing land in a smaller community or a more rural part of Broward, Palm Beach, or surrounding counties, there is a reasonable chance it sits within an eligible boundary — but each address must be confirmed.

**Household income:** USDA sets income limits based on the county and household size. These limits are designed to serve moderate-income borrowers, and they are often more generous than people expect. Your total household income — not just the borrowing borrower's — is considered, so it is worth running the numbers carefully.

## How the Build Process Works

Once you are pre-approved, you will work with a USDA-approved builder. The lender disburses funds to the builder in stages — called draws — as construction milestones are met and inspected. You are not handing money to a contractor upfront and hoping for the best; there is a structured disbursement process that protects everyone involved.

During construction, you typically make interest-only payments on the funds that have been drawn. When the certificate of occupancy is issued and the home is complete, the loan converts automatically to a fully amortizing mortgage. That is the moment your permanent payment kicks in.
