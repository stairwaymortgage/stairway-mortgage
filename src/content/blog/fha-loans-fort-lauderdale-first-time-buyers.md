## What Makes an FHA Loan Different

An FHA loan is a mortgage insured by the Federal Housing Administration. Because the government backs the loan, lenders can extend financing to buyers who might not meet the stricter requirements of a conventional mortgage. That means people with shorter credit histories, modest savings, or a few financial bumps along the way often find FHA to be a realistic route into homeownership.

The two features that tend to matter most to first-time buyers are the down payment and the credit flexibility. FHA loans allow down payments as low as 3.5% in many cases — and unlike conventional loans, that down payment can often come from gift funds. On the credit side, FHA guidelines are generally more forgiving than conventional standards, which matters if you're still building your financial profile.

## What You Need to Know About Down Payments Right Now

One of the most persistent myths in homebuying is that you need 20% down. That figure comes from conventional loan pricing structures, not from a rule carved in stone. Many first-time buyers across South Florida are getting into homes with significantly less.

With an FHA loan, 3.5% down is a widely used starting point for eligible borrowers. On a $350,000 home, that's $12,250 — still a meaningful amount, but far more reachable than $70,000. And if you're combining that with down payment assistance programs available in Broward County, the number you need to bring to the table could shrink further.

## Credit, Income, and Debt: How FHA Looks at the Full Picture

FHA underwriting considers more than your credit score. Lenders look at your debt-to-income ratio, your employment history, and the nature of any past credit issues. A medical collection that's now resolved looks different to an underwriter than a pattern of recent missed payments. Context matters more than many buyers realize.

For income, FHA loans work with W-2 employees, self-employed borrowers, and people who rely on part-time or seasonal work — as long as the income is documented and shows consistency. If you've recently changed jobs within the same field, that typically isn't the red flag it might seem.

## The Property Has to Qualify Too

Here's something many buyers discover mid-process: FHA loans have property standards that not every home will meet. The home needs to be your primary residence, and it must pass an FHA appraisal that checks for health and safety conditions — things like roof integrity, functional utilities, and absence of major structural issues.

In Fort Lauderdale's market, this mostly affects older homes or properties being sold as-is. A home in good condition typically clears the appraisal without issue. But if you're eyeing a fixer-upper, it's worth knowing in advance so you're not caught off guard after you're already under contract.

## Mortgage Insurance: What It Is and Why It Exists

FHA loans come with mortgage insurance premiums — an upfront amount rolled into the loan and an ongoing monthly amount. This is the trade-off for the lower down payment and flexible credit standards. The insurance protects the lender, not you, but it's the mechanism that makes the loan available to a wider range of buyers.

Mortgage insurance is not permanent for all loan types, but with FHA it typically stays for the life of the loan if your down payment is below 10%. That's a real cost to factor into your monthly budget. It doesn't make FHA the wrong choice — for many buyers it's the right one — but you should go in with clear eyes about what you're agreeing to.
