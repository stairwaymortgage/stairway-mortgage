## Buying a Home During Residency in Alachua County Is More Realistic Than You Think

You matched into a residency program at UF Health Shands in Gainesville, you are earning a resident's salary, and you are carrying a mountain of student loan debt. Renting feels like the only option — but it might not be.

Alachua County, home to Gainesville and the University of Florida Health system, draws a steady stream of medical residents each year. The local housing market reflects that mix: you will find everything from historic bungalows near the university to newer construction further out toward Newberry or Archer. Some areas on the outskirts of Alachua County even fall within USDA-eligible rural zones, which opens additional paths to financing.

The challenge is that a conventional mortgage was not built with your situation in mind. Conventional underwriting looks at current income, existing debt, and employment history — three categories where residents often look risky on paper. Physician loan programs exist precisely to close that gap.

## What a Physician Loan Actually Does Differently

A physician loan — sometimes called a doctor loan or medical professional mortgage — is a portfolio product offered by select lenders. It is built around the reality of a medical career rather than a standard W-2 employment path.

Here is what tends to make these programs useful for residents:

**Student loan treatment.** Many physician loan programs use a deferred payment or income-driven repayment calculation when assessing your debt-to-income ratio, rather than a full amortized payment. This can significantly change how much home you may be able to afford.

**Employment contract acceptance.** If you have signed a contract for a fellowship or attending position that starts within a set window — often 60 to 90 days of closing — many lenders will accept that in place of pay stubs.

**No private mortgage insurance.** Conventional loans typically require PMI when a borrower puts down less than 20%. Many physician programs waive this requirement even at lower down payment amounts.

**Flexible down payment options.** Some programs allow as little as 0% down for qualifying borrowers, though putting something down could reduce your overall loan balance.

## Why the Alachua County Market Matters for This Decision

Gainesville is a mid-sized college city with a relatively accessible housing market compared to South Florida metros. That matters because physician loans often have loan limits, and a more affordable market means you are less likely to run into those ceilings as a resident.

At the same time, Alachua County has a mix of urban, suburban, and rural pockets. If you are open to living a few miles outside central Gainesville — in areas like High Springs, Waldo, or parts of the county's western edge — you may find properties that fall within USDA rural development boundaries. A USDA loan carries its own advantages, including no down payment requirement for eligible borrowers and properties.

Not every resident will want a rural setting, of course. Many prefer to be close to UF Health, the VA medical center on Archer Road, or the clinical sites around downtown Gainesville. For those buyers, the physician loan pathway tends to be the most practical route.

## How the Workforce Trend Affects Your Timing

Recent discussions in the medical community point to a growing physician workforce, particularly in primary care. As more residents complete training and transition into attending roles, competition for housing near major academic medical centers — including Gainesville — tends to increase over time.

That does not mean you need to rush into a decision. But it does suggest that locking in a home during residency, while you have a clear training timeline and a predictable schedule, could put you in a more stable position by the time you finish. Buying during residency also means you start building equity earlier, which could matter if you stay in Alachua County for an attending or academic position at UF or one of the regional health systems.

## Common Concerns Residents Have About Buying Now

**"What if I match somewhere else after residency?"**
This is a real consideration. If there is meaningful uncertainty about where you will practice long-term, renting may make more sense. But if you are in a multi-year residency or fellowship at UF Health and have some confidence you will remain in the Gainesville area, purchasing could be a sound financial move.

**"My student debt is enormous."**
Physician loan programs are specifically designed with this reality in mind. Your debt load matters, but it is evaluated differently under these programs than under conventional underwriting.

**"I do not have a 20% down payment."**
Most residents do not. That is not a barrier with the right loan program. Some physician loans allow minimal down payments without the added cost of mortgage insurance.
