## Why Standard Mortgage Guidelines Can Work Against You

Conventional mortgage underwriting was designed around a straightforward income picture: W-2 employment, a steady salary, a debt profile that builds gradually over time. Dentists rarely fit that mold, and the mismatch creates friction.

**Student loan debt** is the most obvious issue. Dental school graduates often carry substantial loan balances, and standard underwriting counts a percentage of that balance as a monthly obligation — even if your actual payment is much smaller under an income-driven plan. That calculation can push your debt-to-income ratio past the threshold some loan programs allow.

**Income trajectory** is another common problem. If you recently finished a residency or fellowship, or if you recently started a new position, your income history may look thin even though your earning potential is strong. Lenders relying strictly on a two-year average may undercount what you actually earn today.

**Practice ownership** adds another layer. Dentists who own their practices often write off legitimate business expenses, which reduces their taxable income. That reduction makes sense from a tax standpoint, but it can make your qualifying income appear lower than your actual cash flow.

None of these situations are disqualifying on their own. They simply call for loan programs and underwriting approaches designed with professionals like you in mind.

## Loan Programs Worth Exploring in Alachua County

Several program types may be worth considering depending on where you are in your career and what kind of property you are purchasing.

**Professional loan programs** — sometimes called physician loans or doctor loans — often extend their eligibility to dentists holding a DDS or DMD. These programs may allow reduced or no down payment requirements, and many handle student loan debt in a way that is more favorable for high-balance borrowers. They are typically designed for primary residence purchases, and they often work with newly employed dentists who can show a signed employment contract even if they have not yet started the position.

You can explore the full range of available options on our [loan programs page](/loan-programs/) to get a broader picture of what might fit your situation.

**Conventional loans** remain a strong option for dentists who have been in practice for two or more years and whose tax returns reflect income in a range that supports the purchase they have in mind. If your financial picture is clean and well-documented, conventional guidelines may serve you well.

**USDA loans** are worth a look if you are considering a property outside Gainesville in one of Alachua County's more rural areas. Parts of the county that sit away from the Gainesville metro may fall within USDA-eligible zones. If the property and your income meet the program requirements, a USDA loan could eliminate the need for a down payment entirely.

## How Self-Employment Affects the Process for Practice Owners

If you own your dental practice — even partially — mortgage underwriting will likely treat you as self-employed. That designation triggers a more detailed income review, typically requiring two years of personal and business tax returns along with a year-to-date profit and loss statement.

The key metric lenders focus on is your net qualifying income after expenses, not your gross revenue. For dentists who run lean on taxable income by design, this can create a gap between what you earn and what underwriting will count.

Some professional loan programs address this by using different documentation standards or by giving more weight to your earnings trajectory. Bank statement loan programs are another avenue some self-employed borrowers explore, using deposit history rather than tax returns to document income.
