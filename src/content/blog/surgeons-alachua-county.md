## When Your Income Is Real but Hard to Document

You have spent the last decade in training — medical school, residency, possibly a fellowship. You are now stepping into an attending role at UF Health or a private surgical practice in or around Gainesville, and you are finally ready to buy a home in Alachua County. There is just one problem: your tax returns look nothing like the income you are actually earning now, and your student loan balance would make most conventional underwriters pause.

This is the situation many surgeons face when they first try to work with a standard lender. The good news is that mortgage programs designed specifically for physicians may offer a path that fits your real financial profile — not just the snapshot your last two years of tax filings show.

---

## What Makes the Surgeon's Financial Profile Unusual

Conventional mortgage underwriting was built around a simple model: steady W-2 income, minimal debt, a few years of stable employment history. Surgeons rarely fit that model at the moment they most need to buy.

A few things make your profile stand out:

**Income timing.** Your earning trajectory often jumps sharply when you move from training to attending. A conventional lender looking backward at your income may not reflect where you are headed.

**Student loans.** Surgical training frequently comes with significant medical school debt. Under conventional guidelines, that balance can affect how much home you appear to qualify for. Physician loan programs often handle deferred or income-driven repayment plans differently, which could open more options.

**Limited down payment savings.** Years of residency and fellowship stipends do not leave much room to build a large cash reserve. Some physician loan programs allow lower down payment amounts without requiring private mortgage insurance, which may reduce your upfront costs.

Understanding [loan programs built for medical professionals](/loan-programs/) is a practical first step before you start touring homes.

## Alachua County: A Market Worth Understanding

Alachua County sits in north-central Florida and is anchored by Gainesville, home to the University of Florida and one of the state's largest academic medical centers. The housing market here reflects that mix of university town, healthcare hub, and surrounding rural character.

Within Gainesville you will find established neighborhoods close to UF Health, newer construction on the city's edges, and a range of townhomes and single-family properties suited to a busy surgeon's schedule. Outside the city, Alachua County opens into smaller communities and rural stretches where properties can be larger and land is more available — and where USDA loan eligibility may apply to certain addresses.

If you are drawn to a home outside Gainesville proper, it is worth checking whether the specific address falls in a USDA-designated area, since that could expand your financing options. Our [loan programs page](/loan-programs/) covers what USDA eligibility generally looks like.

The county has also seen consistent demand from healthcare professionals tied to the university medical system, which means inventory in certain price ranges can move quickly. Being pre-approved and clear on your financing before you make an offer matters here.

## How Physician Loan Programs May Work for You

Physician loan programs — sometimes called doctor loans — are offered by select lenders and are designed with the surgeon's career arc in mind. Here is what they often allow:

- **Use of an employment contract.** If you have a signed offer letter or contract, many programs will use your projected attending income rather than requiring tax returns that reflect residency-level pay.
- **Student loan flexibility.** Deferred medical school debt may be treated more generously than it would be under conventional guidelines.
- **Lower down payment options.** Some programs allow you to put down a smaller amount without requiring private mortgage insurance, though terms vary.
- **Jumbo loan access.** Gainesville's market includes homes that may exceed conventional loan limits, and physician programs often accommodate higher loan amounts.

None of this means you are automatically approved — your full financial picture still matters, and every application is reviewed individually. But the program structure is designed to reflect how physician careers actually work.

## Steps Worth Taking Before You Apply

If you are a surgeon preparing to purchase in Alachua County, a few practical moves can smooth the process:

**Pull your credit report.** Long training periods sometimes mean a thin credit file or accounts that have not been actively managed. Knowing where you stand gives you time to address anything unexpected.

**Gather your employment contract.** If you are transitioning from training to an attending position, your signed offer letter is often more useful than your tax returns at this stage.

**Get a realistic sense of your monthly obligations.** Student loan payments — whether active, deferred, or income-driven — will factor into how lenders calculate your debt picture. Understanding that number ahead of time helps.

**Think about property type.** Are you looking for a Gainesville neighborhood close to the hospital, or are you open to more land outside the city? That choice could affect which loan program makes the most sense.
