## How Firefighter Income Is Viewed by Lenders

Most firefighters do not earn a simple salary. Base pay, overtime, hazard pay, and shift differentials can all be part of the picture. The good news is that many loan programs allow lenders to count these income sources — provided the documentation is in order.

Generally, lenders want to see that variable income has been consistent for at least two years. Pay stubs, W-2s, and sometimes tax returns are used to build that case. When that documentation is organized and presented clearly, firefighters often find that their total qualifying income is stronger than they initially assumed.

If your department has experienced staffing pressures — a reality for many fire stations across Florida right now — you may actually have more overtime income on record than in previous years. That documented history could work in your favor when a lender reviews your file.

## Loan Programs That May Work for Firefighters in Alachua County

Alachua County has a mix of urban, suburban, and rural areas. Gainesville anchors the county as the urban core, but a meaningful portion of the surrounding land sits in less-densely developed zones. That geographic variety opens up more than one path to homeownership.

**USDA Rural Development Loans:** Parts of Alachua County may fall within USDA-eligible boundaries. These government-backed loans could allow qualified borrowers to purchase a home with no down payment, which is a meaningful advantage for first-time buyers watching their savings. Property eligibility is address-specific, so it is worth checking whether the home you are considering qualifies.

**FHA Loans:** FHA loans are a widely used option for first-time homebuyers and often work well for borrowers who are still building their credit profile or who prefer a lower down payment structure. They are not exclusive to any profession, but they can be a practical fit for firefighters entering the market for the first time.

**Conventional Loans:** For firefighters with strong credit and stable documented income, conventional loans may offer additional flexibility in property types and loan structures. Alachua County includes a range of property types — from single-family homes in Gainesville neighborhoods to larger parcels in the county's more rural stretches — and conventional financing can often accommodate that variety.

## First-Time Homebuyers: What to Expect in Alachua County

If this is your first home purchase, the process can feel like a lot to absorb all at once. Alachua County's housing market has its own character — shaped by the presence of the University of Florida, a steady population of long-term residents, and a surrounding rural landscape that appeals to buyers looking for space and quiet.

As a first-time buyer, a few things will matter most early in the process:

- **Your credit profile.** Lenders will review your credit score and payment history. If there are items you want to address before applying, doing that early gives you more time.
- **Your debt-to-income picture.** Lenders look at how your existing monthly obligations compare to your gross income. Firefighters with overtime income may have more room here than they expect.
- **Your available funds.** Down payment and closing costs both need to be accounted for. Some programs reduce or eliminate the down payment requirement, but closing costs are still a factor.
