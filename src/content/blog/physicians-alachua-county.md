## The Financial Picture Most Physicians Face When Buying a Home

You finished medical school, survived residency, and now you are looking at homes in Gainesville or another part of Alachua County. On paper, your income trajectory looks strong. In practice, the mortgage process can feel like it was designed for someone whose financial life looks nothing like yours.

High student loan balances. A short employment history at your current position. Income that may have shifted dramatically from what you earned as a resident. These are the real hurdles physicians face when sitting down with a lender, and they are precisely why specialized loan programs exist.

This post walks through what those programs can offer, how the Alachua County market shapes your choices, and what you should think through before applying.

---

## Why Standard Loan Underwriting Often Creates Problems for Physicians

Conventional mortgage underwriting was built around a fairly simple financial profile: steady W-2 income, a manageable debt load, and a few years of tax returns that tell a consistent story. Most physicians, especially early in their careers, do not fit that mold.

Medical school debt averages well into six figures for most graduates. Underwriting that counts the full projected repayment amount against your monthly income can make your debt-to-income ratio look unfavorable even when your earning capacity is substantial. Some programs designed for physicians treat deferred loans or income-based repayment plans differently, which could open doors that a standard loan would not.

There is also the employment history question. If you just finished residency or fellowship at UF Health Shands in Gainesville and are stepping into your first attending role, you may have limited documentation of your new salary. Physician loan programs often accept a signed offer letter or employment contract as documentation in place of pay stubs, recognizing that your income picture is changing rapidly.

## What Alachua County's Housing Market Means for Your Search

Alachua County is not a single market. Gainesville, the county seat, is a mid-sized city anchored by the University of Florida and a growing healthcare and research economy. Neighborhoods close to UF Health, Shands, and the VA Medical Center tend to carry strong demand and move quickly.

Outside the Gainesville city limits, the county shifts character significantly. Communities like Newberry, Archer, and Alachua itself sit in semi-rural settings where lot sizes are larger, price points tend to be more accessible, and some areas may fall within USDA Rural Development loan eligibility zones. If you have flexibility on location, checking USDA eligibility for specific addresses in Alachua County could expand your options.

Property types vary widely too. You will find historic homes near the university district, newer construction in master-planned communities on the edges of Gainesville, and agricultural-adjacent parcels further out. Each property type can carry different underwriting considerations, particularly if you are using a physician loan program that has its own property guidelines.

## The Student Loan Factor: A Trend Worth Understanding

Physician compensation has been shifting in certain specialties, with competitive hiring in fields that have seen staffing shortages. At the same time, the conversation around student loan relief for medical professionals has remained politically uncertain at the federal level, and state-level programs have had mixed results in terms of reach and funding.

What this means practically: most physicians in Alachua County should plan their home purchase without assuming any future student loan forgiveness will materialize on a specific timeline. The more useful approach is working with a loan program that handles your current debt load in the most realistic way, rather than waiting for an external variable you cannot control.

## Down Payments, PMI, and Physician Loan Structures

One of the most frequently discussed features of physician-specific loan programs is the ability to purchase with a small down payment — sometimes as low as 0% to 10% — without triggering private mortgage insurance. This is not universal across all programs, and specific terms vary by lender, so it is worth confirming exactly what applies to your scenario.

For physicians who are early in their careers, preserving cash rather than committing a large sum to a down payment can make practical sense. Moving costs, setting up a practice or coverage area, and building an emergency fund are all real financial priorities in that first year of attending practice.

That said, putting more down when you have the funds available is worth running through the numbers. Our [mortgage calculators](/calculators/) can help you model different down payment scenarios so you can see how the monthly figures compare before you commit to a direction.

## Self-Employed Physicians and Independent Contractors

Some physicians in Alachua County work in private practice, operate as independent contractors through a group arrangement, or have 1099 income alongside W-2 earnings. If that describes your situation, your mortgage path has an additional layer of complexity.

Lenders reviewing self-employed income typically want to see two years of tax returns to establish a stable income history. If your practice is newer than that, or if aggressive business deductions reduce your reported net income significantly, you may find that your documented income looks lower than your actual cash flow. Some programs allow bank statement documentation as an alternative, which could more accurately reflect what you are actually earning.
