## You Found the Land, But the County Question Stops You Cold

You have been eyeing a piece of property outside the city. Maybe it is in western Broward, a stretch of St. Lucie County, or one of the quieter parcels in Hendry or Glades County. The price is right. The space is exactly what your family needs. But then someone mentions that USDA loans are only for rural areas, and you start wondering whether the county you are looking at even counts.

That confusion is common, and it keeps a lot of first-time buyers from exploring a program that could work well for their situation. The truth is that USDA eligibility in Florida is more nuanced than a simple county-level pass or fail.

## How USDA Eligibility Actually Works by Location

The USDA does not designate entire counties as eligible or ineligible. Instead, eligibility is determined at the individual property address level, using population thresholds and rural character designations maintained by the USDA itself.

This means that a county like Charlotte or Okeechobee might have large swaths of eligible land even though the county seat is a developed town. Similarly, a county that sounds rural might have pockets near a metro area that fall outside the eligible zone.

For Florida specifically, counties worth exploring for USDA construction eligibility often include areas in the following regions:

- **South-central Florida:** Glades, Hendry, and Highlands counties tend to have strong rural eligibility across most of their land area.
- **North Florida:** Counties such as Liberty, Jefferson, Lafayette, and Gilchrist are largely rural and frequently appear on eligible maps.
- **Central Florida fringe areas:** Parts of Polk, Putnam, and Levy counties contain eligible parcels, particularly away from city limits.
- **Treasure Coast and inland areas:** Portions of Okeechobee and Indian River counties may have eligible rural properties depending on proximity to incorporated areas.

## What the USDA Construction-to-Permanent Loan Actually Covers

This is not a traditional purchase loan. The [USDA construction loan program](/loan-programs/usda-construction/) is designed to finance the building of a new primary residence on eligible rural land, combining the construction phase and the permanent mortgage into one loan with a single closing.

That single-close structure matters. It means you go through underwriting once, lock your terms once, and avoid the cost and paperwork of a second closing when construction finishes. For a first-time buyer managing a build for the first time, that simplicity could reduce stress significantly.

Key features of the program that many buyers find useful:

- **Zero down payment requirement.** This is one of very few construction loan programs that does not require a down payment for eligible borrowers.
- **No private mortgage insurance in the traditional sense.** USDA loans use a guarantee fee structure instead, which is built into the loan rather than billed as a separate monthly line item.
- **Fixed terms.** The permanent phase of the loan carries a fixed structure, so you are not left with an adjustable rate once the home is completed.
- **Income limits apply.** USDA loans are intended for low-to-moderate income households, and limits vary by county and household size.

## What You Need to Bring to the Conversation

Before you start planning a build, a few things will help determine whether the USDA construction program is the right fit:

**Your income relative to county limits.** USDA income limits are based on household size and the county where the property is located. Limits in Glades County, for example, differ from limits in Broward County. Your loan officer will check this early in the process.

**The property address or parcel.** Even before you make an offer, running the address through the eligibility map costs nothing and takes minutes. If the parcel is not eligible, you will know before you invest time in the application.

**A licensed general contractor.** USDA construction loans require that the build be completed by a licensed contractor. Owner-builder arrangements are generally not permitted under this program.

**A realistic build timeline.** Construction loans typically have a defined window for the build to be completed. Understanding that window upfront helps you choose the right builder and set realistic expectations.
