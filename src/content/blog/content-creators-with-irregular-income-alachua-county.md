## How Lenders Read a Creator's Income

Most conventional loan programs look at two years of self-employment income, averaged. They use your tax returns — specifically Schedule C or Schedule K-1 if you operate through an entity — and in many cases they subtract business expenses before calculating what counts.

That last part surprises people. If you wrote off a home studio, equipment, travel, and software last year, your taxable income on paper might be lower than your actual cash flow. That is not a problem unique to creators, but it is one worth planning around before you apply.

Some programs offer an alternative: bank statement loans, which use twelve or twenty-four months of deposits to calculate income rather than tax returns. These programs may suit creators whose books show strong cash flow even after write-offs. They often require a larger down payment and work differently from conventional financing, but they exist specifically for borrowers in your situation.

## What You Can Do Right Now to Strengthen Your File

Before you start searching Alachua County listings seriously, a few habits now can make your mortgage file easier to work with later.

**Keep business and personal finances separate.** A dedicated business account makes it straightforward to show lenders exactly what came in and what went out. Mixed accounts create questions.

**File your taxes on time — and accurately.** Many creators wait to file or use extensions, which is fine legally. But lenders need your most recent two years of returns. If one year is missing, it can delay or complicate your application.

**Document every income stream.** Brand deal contracts, 1099 forms, platform earnings statements, and client invoices all help tell the story of a stable business, even when the monthly totals vary.

**Understand your debt picture.** Student loans, car payments, and credit card balances all affect your debt-to-income ratio, which lenders use to assess what monthly mortgage payment your income can support.
