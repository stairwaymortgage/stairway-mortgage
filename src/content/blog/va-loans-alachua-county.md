## What the VA Loan Program Actually Offers

The VA loan program is a federal benefit backed by the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs. Eligible veterans, active-duty service members, and surviving spouses may be able to use it to purchase a primary residence without a down payment in many cases.

Here are some of the program's most recognized features:

- **No down payment** in most qualifying scenarios
- **No private mortgage insurance (PMI)**, which is a monthly cost required on many conventional loans
- **A funding fee** that varies based on your service history and loan use — this can sometimes be rolled into the loan amount
- **Flexible credit guidelines** compared to many conventional options
- **The ability to reuse the benefit** more than once in many cases

For veterans in Alachua County who may be balancing student loan debt, a growing family, or the costs of transitioning out of service, the ability to skip a large down payment could open doors that might otherwise stay closed for years.

## Understanding Alachua County's Housing Market as a Veteran Buyer

Alachua County sits in north-central Florida, anchored by Gainesville — a city with a large university presence, a steady rental market, and a mix of older neighborhoods and newer subdivisions. Outside Gainesville, the county shifts quickly toward more rural character, with small towns, agricultural land, and wooded properties.

This mix matters for VA loan purposes for a few reasons.

First, VA loans require the property to meet minimum property requirements (MPRs). Older homes and rural properties sometimes surface issues during the VA appraisal process — things like roof condition, well and septic functionality, or structural concerns. These are worth knowing about before you make an offer, not after. A recent conversation around this nationally involved buyers discovering that a home's aging roof became a sticking point during the VA loan process. In Alachua County's older housing stock, this is a real scenario to prepare for.

Second, some rural parts of Alachua County may also be eligible for USDA loan programs, which gives veterans an additional option to consider if they are open to properties in qualifying areas. Our [loan programs overview](/loan-programs/) can help you compare what might apply to your situation.

Third, Gainesville's market draws buyers at multiple price points, including first-time buyers and those relocating for work or family. VA loans can be used across a wide range of home prices, subject to county loan limits and lender guidelines.

## The Assumable Loan Question: What Veterans in Alachua County Should Know

Assumable VA loans have drawn real interest lately, and for understandable reasons. When a seller has an existing VA loan, a qualified buyer may be able to assume that loan — meaning they step into the seller's loan rather than obtaining new financing at current terms.

This could, in some cases, result in a monthly payment that looks meaningfully different from what new financing would produce. However, there are real limitations:

- The assuming buyer must typically meet VA and lender qualification standards
- The veteran seller's VA loan entitlement may remain tied up until the loan is paid off or refinanced, unless a veteran-to-veteran assumption is structured correctly
- Not every seller or real estate agent fully understands how to navigate this process

If you come across a listing in Alachua County where assumable financing is mentioned, it is worth a detailed conversation before you factor it into your plans. ## Common Missteps That Can Slow Down a VA Purchase

Veterans who are not familiar with how VA loans differ from conventional financing sometimes run into avoidable delays. A few patterns that come up frequently:

**Skipping the Certificate of Eligibility early.** Your COE confirms your VA eligibility and is a required document in the loan process. Getting it early removes one variable from your timeline.

**Not budgeting for closing costs.** VA loans do not require a down payment, but closing costs are still real. In some cases, sellers can contribute to these costs, but that is something to negotiate — not assume.

**Choosing a home without considering VA appraisal requirements.** A standard home inspection and a VA appraisal are two different things. Properties with deferred maintenance, older roofs, or non-functional systems may hit snags during the VA appraisal. In Alachua County's older residential areas, this is a detail worth discussing with your agent and loan officer before submitting an offer.

**Not understanding the funding fee.** The VA funding fee can vary based on several factors. Some veterans with qualifying service-connected disabilities may be exempt. Understanding this before closing helps you plan.
