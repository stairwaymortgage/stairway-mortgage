## Building a Home in Baker County Without a Large Down Payment

You have found a piece of land in Baker County — maybe outside Macclenny, maybe tucked along one of the quiet rural roads that define this part of northeast Florida. You know what you want to build. What you may not know is whether you can afford to build it without draining your savings.

For buyers in rural areas like Baker County, the USDA One-Time Close Construction Loan is one of the most practical paths to building a home from the ground up. It combines construction financing and a permanent mortgage into a single loan — and in many cases, it requires no down payment at all.

This post walks you through how it works, who it is designed for, and what you should expect if you decide to pursue it.

## What Is a USDA One-Time Close Construction Loan?

The USDA One-Time Close loan is a government-backed program designed specifically for borrowers building in rural and suburban areas. It is not a product for urban construction. It exists because the USDA Rural Development program recognizes that rural communities — like Baker County — need pathways to homeownership that reflect their land availability and housing market realities.

Here is what makes this loan structure worth understanding:

- **Single closing.** You close once, before construction begins. There is no second closing when the home is complete.
- **No down payment required in most cases.** Eligible borrowers may finance up to 100% of the appraised value of the completed home.
- **One loan, one rate, one set of terms.** Your permanent mortgage is locked in at the time of closing, so you are not re-qualifying or re-applying when construction wraps up.
- **Builder approval is required.** The contractor you choose must meet USDA guidelines, which Jim Blackburn, NMLS #1072866, can help you navigate from the beginning.

## How the Process Works, Step by Step

A One-Time Close loan does not work exactly like a standard purchase mortgage. There are more moving parts, and understanding the sequence upfront will help you avoid surprises.

**1. Pre-qualification.** Before anything else, you need to understand what loan amount you may be eligible for. Income limits apply to USDA loans, and your debt-to-income profile matters.

**2. Lot and builder selection.** You will need both an eligible property and an approved builder. These two elements need to be in place before underwriting can move forward.

**3. Construction plans and appraisal.** The lender orders an appraisal based on the completed value of the home — using your construction plans and specs. This "as-completed" appraisal determines the maximum loan amount.

**4. Single closing.** Once everything is approved, you close once. Construction funding is released in draws as work progresses. You typically pay interest only on disbursed funds during the build phase.

**5. Modification to permanent loan.** When construction is complete and the home passes inspection, the loan automatically converts to a standard permanent mortgage. No second closing, no re-qualification.

## Who This Loan Is Designed For

The USDA One-Time Close Construction Loan is not for everyone, but for the right borrower in the right location, it can open a door that would otherwise stay closed.

This program may be a strong fit if you:

- Are building in a USDA-eligible area like much of Baker County
- Have steady income but limited savings for a down payment
- Want to avoid the complexity and cost of two separate closings
- Are working with a licensed general contractor ready to meet USDA requirements
- Fall within USDA household income limits for your area

It may not be the right path if you plan to build a very large or high-cost home, if your income exceeds program limits, or if your chosen lot is outside a designated rural zone.

## What to Watch Out For During the Process

Building a home is a longer process than buying an existing one. There are a few things that catch borrowers off guard when using a One-Time Close structure.

**Builder vetting takes time.** Not every contractor has worked with USDA construction loans before. Finding one who is willing to meet the documentation and draw schedule requirements is an important early step.

**The timeline matters.** USDA construction loans typically have a maximum construction period. If your build runs long, it can create complications. Understanding your builder's realistic timeline before closing is critical.

**Cost overruns are not automatically covered.** The loan is based on the approved construction budget. If costs increase during the build, that gap may need to be covered out of pocket. A detailed, realistic construction budget at the outset protects you.

**Income limits apply.** USDA loans are designed for low-to-moderate income households. There are maximum income thresholds based on household size and location. These limits are adjusted periodically, and they apply to total household income — not just the borrower's income.
