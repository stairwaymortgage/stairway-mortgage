## When Your Paycheck Is More Complicated Than It Looks

You spend your shifts managing separation between aircraft at speeds and altitudes most people never think about. The last thing you want is to spend your days off untangling mortgage paperwork that doesn't account for how you actually get paid.

Air traffic controllers employed by the FAA receive base pay, locality pay, night differential, overtime, and in some cases administrative premium pay — all in the same paycheck. That combination is real income, but it doesn't always fit neatly into the boxes a standard mortgage application expects. That mismatch is one of the most common friction points controllers run into when trying to purchase or refinance a home.

Understanding how lenders view that income — and how to document it correctly from the start — can make the entire process feel far less frustrating.

## How Lenders Read Your FAA Pay Stubs

Most mortgage lenders want to see a two-year history of any income they plan to count. For air traffic controllers, that means base pay is usually straightforward. The complexity starts with the variable portions.

Locality pay, which adjusts your salary based on the cost of living in your facility's geographic area, is typically treated as stable income. Night differential and overtime are handled differently. A lender will generally average those earnings over 24 months, then apply that average to your qualifying income — but only if the history is consistent. If you recently transferred facilities or moved from a lower-pay band to a higher one, the documentation needs to reflect that change clearly.

Federal pay stubs, Leave and Earnings Statements (LES documents for those who came up through military service), and IRS tax transcripts all play a role. Gathering these early, and making sure they tell a coherent story, is worth doing before you ever talk to a real estate agent.

## The Staffing Situation and What It Means for Your Housing Plans

Recent reporting has highlighted staffing shortfalls at air traffic facilities across the country. For individual controllers, that environment often means more available overtime — which can meaningfully affect how much income a lender is able to count on your behalf.

At the same time, facility transfers, reassignments, and promotion to higher certification levels can create gaps or jumps in your income record that look confusing on paper. A lender reviewing your file needs context, not just numbers.

If you are currently in training, recently certified at a new facility, or expecting a significant pay change because of a staffing-driven reassignment, it is worth discussing the timing of a home purchase with someone who understands how those transitions affect documentation. Rushing into an application before your income history reflects your current situation could work against you.

## Down Payments, Reserves, and the Federal Pay Cycle

Federal employees are paid on a biweekly schedule. That rhythm is predictable, which lenders tend to view positively. Still, saving for a down payment while managing the cost of living in areas like South Florida — where many controllers work at TRACON and ARTCC facilities — requires planning.

Depending on the loan program, down payment requirements can vary. Some programs allow as little as 3% to 5% down for qualified borrowers, while others may require more. Reserves — meaning the funds left in your account after closing — are also evaluated by most lenders. Having two to three months of housing expenses set aside beyond the down payment could support your application.

## VA Loans: A Separate Path for Controllers Who Served

Not all air traffic controllers are civilians. A meaningful number worked in military aviation — Army, Navy, Air Force, or Marine Corps — before transitioning to FAA employment. If that describes you, and you meet VA loan eligibility requirements based on your service, that program deserves serious consideration.

VA loans are available to eligible veterans and active-duty service members and come with distinct documentation and underwriting characteristics. If you are unsure whether your service record makes you eligible, the [air traffic controllers resource page](/aviation-professionals/air-traffic-controllers/) on this site outlines some of the key considerations.

For controllers who entered the FAA directly without prior military service, VA loans are not an available path — but there are several other programs worth reviewing.
