## When Your Business Success Does Not Translate on Paper

You have built something real. Your business generates consistent revenue, you manage cash flow each month, and you have been at it long enough to know this is your livelihood — not a side project. Then you sit down to apply for a home loan in Alachua County and discover that the tax return your accountant optimized to minimize what you owe the IRS is now working against you.

This is one of the most common frustrations for business owners and entrepreneurs seeking a mortgage. The strategies that serve you well at tax time — deducting equipment, writing off a home office, running expenses through the business — reduce your documented net income, which is exactly what traditional lenders use to assess your ability to repay a loan.

Understanding how lenders view self-employed income, and which loan programs are structured to work with it, can make a significant difference in what you are able to accomplish as a borrower.

## How Lenders Read Self-Employment Income

When a salaried employee applies for a mortgage, the math is relatively straightforward. When you apply as a business owner, lenders typically average two years of net income from your Schedule C, partnership returns, or S-corp filings — and then they add back certain deductions like depreciation. The result is called qualifying income, and it may be lower than what your actual monthly deposits reflect.

Lenders also look at whether your business income is stable or declining year over year. If your revenue dipped in one of the two years being averaged, that can affect what you may be approved for. It is not a disqualifier in most cases, but it is something you want to understand before you apply.

## Loan Programs Worth Knowing About

Not every mortgage product on the market assumes you receive a W-2. Several programs are built with business owners in mind, and knowing they exist can open doors that a standard conventional application might not.

**Bank Statement Loans**
Instead of tax returns, these programs allow lenders to average 12 or 24 months of personal or business bank deposits to calculate qualifying income. This approach often reflects what self-employed borrowers actually earn more accurately than a Schedule C does. Explore the full range of programs available through our [loan programs](/loan-programs/) overview.

**Profit and Loss Statement Loans**
Some lenders will accept a CPA-prepared P&L statement — sometimes covering as little as one year — as the primary income document. This can be useful if your business is newer or if your most recent tax return does not reflect current earnings.

**Conventional Loans with Strong Documentation**
If your net income after add-backs is sufficient, a conventional loan may still serve you well. These often carry standard documentation requirements but provide access to a wide range of property types across Alachua County, including single-family homes and investment properties.

**USDA Loans**
Portions of Alachua County outside of Gainesville may fall within USDA-designated rural eligible areas. If you are considering a property in one of those zones, a USDA loan could be worth exploring depending on your income and the property's eligibility. ## What Documentation You Should Start Gathering

Regardless of which loan type fits your situation, preparation is the single most powerful thing you can do before applying. Business owners who walk into the process organized move through underwriting with far fewer delays.

Here is what lenders commonly request from self-employed borrowers:

- Two years of personal federal tax returns, all schedules
- Two years of business tax returns (if applicable)
- Year-to-date profit and loss statement
- 12 to 24 months of business and personal bank statements
- Documentation of business ownership, such as a business license or CPA letter confirming self-employment

If you are pursuing a bank statement loan, the quality and consistency of your deposit history matters. Large irregular deposits, transfers between accounts, or co-mingled funds can create questions in underwriting. A loan advisor can help you review your statements in advance and flag anything that may need explanation.
