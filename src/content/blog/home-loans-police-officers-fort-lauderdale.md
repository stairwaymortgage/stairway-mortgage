## Your Income Is More Than Your Base Salary

Across the country, law enforcement agencies have been raising starting pay and expanding hiring as departments work to staff up. That's meaningful for officers entering the profession — and it also matters to lenders reviewing your application.

But overtime is where many officers quietly build significant annual income. If you've been logging consistent overtime hours over the past two years, that income may be includable when lenders calculate your qualifying amount. It doesn't always count automatically — lenders typically want to see that it's documented, consistent, and likely to continue.

Other income sources that could strengthen your application:

- Off-duty security or law enforcement detail work
- A working spouse or co-borrower's income
- Reserve or National Guard pay, if applicable
- Any rental income you currently receive

Gathering two years of W-2s, pay stubs, and tax returns before you start shopping for a home could help your loan officer give you a realistic picture of what you may qualify for.

## Programs Built for Public Servants

Police officers often have access to loan programs and assistance funds that aren't widely advertised. These aren't automatic — eligibility depends on your income, the county or city where you're buying, and the loan type — but they're worth knowing about.

In Florida, certain state and local programs are specifically designed for first responders and public servants. Some offer down payment assistance, which could reduce the cash required at closing. Others allow the down payment itself to be as low as 3% or 3.5% of the purchase price, depending on the loan program.

## Common Questions Officers Ask Before Getting Started

**Does a second job affect my application?**
Generally, yes — in a positive way. If you've held a second job consistently for at least two years, that income may be counted alongside your primary employment income. Documentation is the key.

**What if I'm still in my probationary period?**
Some loan programs require a minimum employment history. If you recently joined the force, it may make sense to wait until you've completed your probationary period and have more stable, documented income history. Jim Blackburn, NMLS #1072866, can walk you through timelines based on your current situation.

**What happens if I transfer to a different agency or department?**
Lenders look at employment stability. A transfer within the same profession — law enforcement to law enforcement — is typically viewed differently than leaving the field entirely. It's worth discussing the specifics before you apply.

**Can I buy a home while carrying student loans or a car payment?**
Most first-time buyers carry some existing debt. Lenders look at your debt-to-income ratio, which compares your monthly debt obligations to your monthly income. Depending on your numbers, you may still qualify for a loan that works for your situation.
