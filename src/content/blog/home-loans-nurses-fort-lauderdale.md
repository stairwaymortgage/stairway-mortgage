## Why Nursing Income Is Often Misread by Generic Lenders

Many nurses earn a base hourly wage but take home significantly more through overtime, night or weekend differentials, and specialty pay. The challenge is documentation. A lender who is not familiar with healthcare compensation may discount or exclude those amounts, which reduces your apparent income on paper and could limit the loan amount you might access.

If you are a travel nurse or work per diem, the picture becomes even more layered. Your income may come through a staffing agency, a 1099, or a combination of both. Some assignments include housing stipends or meal allowances that are not taxable — which means they may not appear in a traditional income calculation at all.

Nurse practitioners face a slightly different issue. Your salary may be higher and more consistent, but if you recently transitioned from RN to NP — or moved from a hospital to a private practice or telehealth role — the employment history piece of the file requires careful handling.

These are solvable situations. But they do require a loan originator who takes the time to actually read your documentation rather than running it through an automated filter and moving on.

## Loan Programs That May Work Well for Nurses

There is no single mortgage product designed exclusively for nurses, but several programs may align well with how nursing income is structured and what nurses typically need.

**FHA loans** are often accessible for first-time buyers with moderate down payments and may be more flexible on credit history. If your income documentation is complex, an FHA pathway is worth exploring.

**Conventional loans** may allow higher loan limits and could be appropriate if your income history is well-documented and your credit profile is strong. Nurse practitioners who have been in stable salaried positions for two or more years often find conventional financing straightforward.

**Bank statement or alternative documentation loans** exist for nurses whose income is variable, contract-based, or primarily 1099. These programs look at actual cash flow rather than tax return income and may open doors that standard programs would not.

**Down payment assistance programs** at the state and county level are sometimes available to healthcare workers, including nurses, particularly for first-time buyers. Availability changes, but Jim Blackburn, NMLS #1072866, stays current on what might be active in Broward County.

## How the Process Works When You Have a Complicated File

If your income situation is not straightforward, the process does not have to be chaotic. It just needs to be organized from the beginning.

The first step is usually gathering two years of employment history and income documentation — W-2s, pay stubs, tax returns, or agency contracts depending on how you are paid. If you have had multiple employers, that is not automatically a problem, especially in healthcare where hospital transitions are common.

From there, the file gets structured in a way that tells your story clearly. Lenders want to see that income is stable and likely to continue — not that it looks exactly like every other borrower's income.
