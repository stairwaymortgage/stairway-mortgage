## What Makes the VA Loan Different for Active Duty Buyers

The VA loan is not a one-size-fits-all product, but it does carry several structural features that many active duty buyers find useful.

First, eligible borrowers may be able to purchase a home with no down payment required. That matters when you are managing a military salary, relocating costs, and the expenses of setting up a new household. Rather than spending years building a down payment fund, you may be able to move forward sooner.

Second, VA loans do not require private mortgage insurance, which is a monthly cost that non-VA borrowers typically pay when they put less than 20% down. Removing that cost from your monthly payment can make a meaningful difference in how the numbers work for your budget.

Third, the VA loan program has guidelines designed with military life in mind — including allowances for deployments, periods of non-occupancy, and income verification for service members whose pay structure includes allowances and special pays.

## Navigating PCS Moves and Loan Timing

One of the questions Jim Blackburn, NMLS #1072866, hears most often from active duty buyers is: "What if I get new orders right after I close?"

It is a fair concern. The reality is that many service members have handled exactly this situation. If orders come through after you purchase, you have several paths — renting the home to cover the mortgage, selling if the market supports it, or holding the property as a long-term asset.

Timing your purchase relative to your expected time at a duty station does matter. If you have two or more years remaining at your current station, buying often makes more sense than if you are months away from a known rotation. A loan originator who understands military life can help you think through that timing honestly.

## How Your Military Income Is Evaluated

Military pay is structured differently than civilian income, and that structure affects how lenders evaluate your loan application. Base pay is the foundation, but many service members also receive Basic Allowance for Housing (BAH), Basic Allowance for Subsistence (BAS), special pays, and re-enlistment bonuses.

Not all of these income types are treated the same way in underwriting. BAH, for example, is often counted as stable, non-taxable income that may strengthen your overall application. Special pays and bonuses may or may not be included depending on their documented history and likelihood of continuation.

The key is working with a loan originator who knows how to present military income accurately and completely. Mistakes or omissions in how your income is documented can slow down the process or create unnecessary obstacles that could have been avoided from the start.
