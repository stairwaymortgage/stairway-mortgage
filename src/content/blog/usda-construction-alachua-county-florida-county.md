## You Own the Land. Now What?

You bought a few acres outside Gainesville years ago. Maybe it was an investment. Maybe you always pictured a house there eventually. Now that time is here, and you are staring at a question that stumps a lot of Alachua County landowners: how do you actually finance building a home on raw rural land?

Conventional construction loans can be complicated, require significant cash reserves, and may not fit the unique character of rural parcels. But there is a program specifically designed for situations like yours — the USDA Single Close Construction Loan — and it is worth understanding before you assume this dream is out of reach.

---

## What Makes Alachua County a Strong Fit for This Program

Alachua County covers a wide footprint in North Central Florida. Gainesville anchors the county as its seat, but much of the surrounding land — places like Newberry, Archer, Hawthorne, and areas east toward Waldo — carries a distinctly rural character. Large residential lots, agricultural parcels, and wooded tracts are common.

The USDA defines eligible areas based on population thresholds, and a meaningful portion of Alachua County falls within those boundaries. That means if your land sits outside the denser suburban zones around Gainesville, there is a real possibility your parcel could qualify for USDA financing. This is one of the reasons the program is worth exploring early, rather than assuming your property does not fit.

## How the USDA Single Close Construction Loan Works

The name says a lot. Instead of juggling a short-term construction loan and then refinancing into a permanent mortgage when the build is done, you close once. One set of closing costs. One approval process. One loan that converts automatically to a standard mortgage when your home is complete.

During the build phase, the lender disburses funds in draws as construction milestones are met. Once the certificate of occupancy is issued and the home passes final inspection, your loan converts to a 30-year fixed mortgage — no second closing required.

For rural landowners in Alachua County who are already carrying costs on their land, this structure can simplify the financial picture considerably.

Here are a few key features of the program:

- **No down payment required** in most cases for eligible borrowers and properties
- **One closing** covers both the construction phase and permanent financing
- **Land equity** you already hold may be applied toward the transaction
- **Owner-occupied primary residences only** — this is not a program for investment or vacation properties
- **Income limits apply** — USDA programs are designed for low-to-moderate income households

## The Qualification Picture

USDA construction loans have specific eligibility requirements on both the borrower and property side. Meeting them is not automatic, but many Alachua County landowners find they are closer to qualifying than they expected.

**Property eligibility:** Your land must be in a USDA-designated rural area. The USDA maintains an online eligibility map, and Jim Blackburn, NMLS #1072866, can help you run your specific parcel address through that tool.

**Income eligibility:** USDA programs set household income limits that vary by county and household size. These limits are designed to serve moderate-income families — not just the lowest income tiers — so a working household with steady income may still fit within the program's guidelines.

**Credit and debt:** There is no hard published minimum credit score for USDA loans, though most lenders look for a score in the mid-600s or above, and your overall debt-to-income ratio will be evaluated. A credit profile with a solid payment history and manageable debt load puts you in a stronger position.

**Builder approval:** Your chosen contractor must be licensed, insured, and accepted by the lender as a USDA-approved builder. This step takes time, so starting builder conversations early is worth doing.
