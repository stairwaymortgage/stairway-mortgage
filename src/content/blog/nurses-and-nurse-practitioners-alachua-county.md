## When Your Schedule Is Unpredictable, Your Home Loan Doesn't Have to Be

You work long shifts, rotate between units, and sometimes pick up extra hours when the floor is short-staffed. Your income reflects that — base pay, shift differentials, overtime, and in some cases per-diem contracts layered on top. When you sit down with a standard mortgage application, it can feel like the form was designed for someone who works nine to five and has never seen a weekend call.

If you are a registered nurse, LPN, or nurse practitioner living or working in Alachua County, Florida, this guide is written for you. The housing market in and around Gainesville — the county seat — has a distinct character shaped by the University of Florida Health system, Shands Hospital, and the surrounding communities that stretch into more rural parts of the county. That mix creates real options for nurses at different income levels and career stages, and understanding those options starts with understanding how your income is evaluated.

## Why Nursing Income Looks Different to a Lender

Most lenders are built to evaluate salaried W-2 employees with a single, steady paycheck. Nursing income often looks different on paper even when it is substantial and reliable.

Shift differentials, overtime, and agency or per-diem pay are all legitimate income sources, but they require specific documentation to be counted. A lender typically wants to see a two-year history of that income appearing on your W-2s or pay stubs before averaging it into your qualifying amount. If you recently moved from staff nursing to a nurse practitioner role with a higher base salary, that transition matters too — and how it is handled can significantly affect which programs you may access.

The good news is that these are solvable problems when you work with someone who understands how clinical careers are structured. ## What the Alachua County Market Looks Like for Nurses

Alachua County is a mix of urban density near Gainesville and quieter, semi-rural communities to the north and south. That geography matters for your home search in a few specific ways.

In Gainesville itself, you will find single-family homes, townhomes, and condominiums within a reasonable commute of the major hospital systems. Properties here tend to move steadily given the stable employment base the university and healthcare sector provide.

Outside the Gainesville city limits — in areas like Newberry, Alachua, or Archer — properties are often more spread out and may fall within USDA Rural Development loan eligibility zones. A USDA loan could allow eligible buyers to purchase with no down payment, which is a meaningful option for nurses who are carrying student loan debt or simply want to preserve savings. Eligibility depends on the property address and household income, so it is worth checking before you assume a property qualifies or does not.

## Nurse Practitioners: Newer Income, Strong Trajectory

If you recently completed your NP credentials and moved into a higher-paying role, you may be in a position that feels financially strong but looks complicated on paper. Lenders generally want to see two years of self-employment history if you work independently, or a consistent employment record if you are hospital-employed.

A newer NP role does not necessarily close doors, but it does require a thoughtful approach to documentation. An offer letter, employment contract, and clear evidence of your license and specialty can all support your file. In many cases, nurse practitioners with less than two years in their current role have still been able to move forward with the right loan structure.

The staffing conversations happening across the healthcare industry — with nurses advocating loudly for pay equity and sustainable workloads — reflect a broader recognition that clinical professionals are high-value workers. Mortgage lenders who specialize in medical professionals recognize that too, and loan structures exist that account for career trajectory rather than just the last 24 months of tax returns.

## Down Payment Options Worth Knowing About

One of the most common concerns nurses raise is the down payment. Between student loans from nursing school or NP programs and the cost of living in a college town like Gainesville, saving a large lump sum while renting can feel like a treadmill.

Here are the main program types that may apply:

- **FHA loans** typically require a down payment of 3.5%, and income documentation flexibility can work in a nurse's favor.
- **Conventional loans** may allow as little as 3% down with strong credit, and they do not carry the lifetime mortgage insurance requirement that FHA loans do in some scenarios.
- **USDA loans** may allow zero down payment for eligible properties in qualifying rural areas of Alachua County.
- **VA loans** are available for nurses and NPs who have qualifying military service — this is separate from any civilian nursing benefit.

## How to Get Your File Ready

The nurses who move through the homebuying process most smoothly are usually the ones who come in organized. Here is what to gather before your first conversation:

- Two years of W-2s from all employers
- Recent pay stubs covering at least 30 days
- Two years of federal tax returns if you have any self-employment or 1099 income
- Documentation of any per-diem or contract arrangements
- Your NP license and any employment offer letters if you recently changed roles
- A current picture of your student loan repayment terms

The student loan piece matters more than many nurses expect. Income-driven repayment plans can affect how lenders calculate your debt obligations, and the specific plan you are on could influence which loan programs are accessible to you.
