## What Makes the VA Loan Different From Other Programs

The VA loan program was created specifically to help eligible veterans, active-duty service members, and surviving spouses purchase homes. A few characteristics set it apart from conventional and FHA options.

**No down payment in many cases.** Eligible borrowers may be able to purchase a home without putting money down, which removes one of the biggest obstacles to homeownership for many veterans.

**No private mortgage insurance.** Conventional loans typically require PMI when the down payment is below 20%. VA loans do not carry this requirement, which can meaningfully reduce your monthly housing costs.

**Funding fee instead of mortgage insurance.** VA loans do carry a one-time funding fee that helps sustain the program. The amount varies based on factors like whether it is your first use of the benefit and whether you make a down payment. Certain veterans with service-connected disabilities may be exempt from this fee entirely.

**Flexible credit approach.** The VA does not set a minimum credit score, though individual lenders may have their own overlays. Borrowers with credit challenges may still find pathways forward through the VA program that might not exist elsewhere.

## Who May Be Eligible for a VA Loan

Eligibility is determined by the Department of Veterans Affairs based on your service history. Generally, you may be eligible if you fall into one of these categories:

- You served on active duty for a minimum period and were discharged under conditions other than dishonorable
- You served in the National Guard or Reserves and meet specific service requirements
- You are the surviving spouse of a veteran who died in service or from a service-connected disability

To use your VA benefit, you will need to obtain a Certificate of Eligibility, or COE, from the VA. This document confirms to the lender that you meet the service requirements for the program. In many cases, a lender can pull this document on your behalf during the application process.

## The VA Loan Process: What to Expect

For many veterans, the loan process feels unfamiliar, especially if this is your first home purchase. Here is a general outline of how it tends to work.

**Step one: Establish your eligibility.** This means obtaining your COE and reviewing your service history. Your loan officer handles much of this for you.

**Step two: Understand your financial picture.** Your lender will look at your income, employment, debt load, and credit profile to determine what loan amount you may be able to work with.

**Step three: Find a property.** You will work with a real estate agent to identify homes that meet your needs and are likely to pass the VA appraisal process.

**Step four: VA appraisal.** This is separate from a home inspection. A VA-assigned appraiser will assess the property's value and ensure it meets the VA's minimum property requirements.

**Step five: Underwriting and closing.** Once the appraisal clears and your file is complete, the loan moves through underwriting. If approved, you will schedule a closing date.

## Common Questions Veterans Ask Before Applying

**Can I use my VA benefit if I already used it once?**

In most cases, yes. VA entitlement can often be restored once a prior VA loan is paid off, or in some situations, you may have remaining entitlement available to use on a second property. This is something worth discussing with your loan officer early in the conversation.

**What if my credit is not perfect?**

The VA loan program is designed with some flexibility built in. While lenders do look at credit history, the absence of a mortgage insurance requirement and the backing of the VA can make this program more accessible to those with past credit challenges than many other options.

**Can I use a VA loan to buy a multi-unit property?**

Yes, in many cases. VA loans may be used to purchase properties with up to four units, provided you occupy one of them as your primary residence. This can be a way for veterans to generate rental income while using a benefit they already earned.
