## Why Income Documentation Is the First Hurdle

Most aircraft mechanics are W-2 employees, which sounds straightforward. The complication is that a large share of your earnings may come from sources beyond your base hourly wage: overtime, shift differentials, tool allowances, hazard pay, or per diem. Each of those income types is handled differently in a mortgage underwriter's calculation.

Overtime and shift-differential pay, for example, typically must be averaged over a two-year period before a lender will count it. If you recently moved to a higher-overtime role or changed employers, that two-year clock may need to restart — or there may be exceptions worth exploring.

Per diem and expense reimbursements are usually excluded from qualifying income entirely. Tool allowances depend on how they appear on your pay stub and tax documents. Understanding which dollars count, and proving they are stable, is the core documentation task for [aircraft mechanics exploring home loan options](/aviation-professionals/aircraft-mechanics/).

## Shift Work, Irregular Schedules, and What Underwriters Want to See

Aviation maintenance does not run on a Monday-through-Friday schedule, and neither does your income. Underwriters know this in theory, but your file still needs to tell a clear, documented story.

Here is what typically strengthens an aircraft mechanic's mortgage application:

**Two years of W-2s.** These show your total annual earnings and make it easy to establish an average, especially when overtime is a significant portion of your income.

**Recent pay stubs.** Usually the most recent 30 days, showing year-to-date totals that align with your W-2 history.

**A steady employer history.** Staying in the aviation maintenance field — even if you change employers — tends to read well to underwriters because it demonstrates career continuity in a licensed trade.

**Verification of employment.** Most lenders will contact your employer directly, so having your HR or payroll contact information ready speeds things up.

If you have side contract work — doing A&P inspections, ferry flights, or independent maintenance gigs — that income requires two years of self-employment tax returns and may also need a profit-and-loss statement.
